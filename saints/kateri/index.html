👋 Meet Kateri

  • 👶 Born in 1656 in the Mohawk village of Ossernenon, in what is now New York State.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧 Her father was a Mohawk chief. Her mother was an Algonquin woman who was a Christian.
  • 💧 She was baptized in 1676 and took the name Kateri (Catherine).
  • 🛶 She moved to Kahnawake, a Christian Mohawk community near Montreal, Canada.
  • 🌸 She died in 1680, at just 24 years old. She is called the “Lily of the Mohawks.”
  • 😇 In 2012, she became the first Native American saint from the United States and Canada.
😇 What is a saint? A saint is a person who loved God and other people in a heroic way, and who the Catholic Church believes is now in heaven. Catholics ask the saints to pray for them — just like you might ask a friend to pray for you!

🌟 You Can Be Like Kateri!

  • 🙏 Faith: say a short prayer when you wake up.
  • 🦁 Courage: do the right thing, even when it's hard.
  • ✝️ Prayer: find a quiet spot and talk to God like a friend.
  • 💛 Kindness: help someone who is sick, sad, or needs a hand.
  • 🌱 Patience: stay gentle, even when things are hard.
  • 🌳 Love of creation: take care of plants, animals, and nature.

😇 The Road to Sainthood

Becoming a saint takes a long time! The Church carefully studies a person's life, and there are special steps along the way.

Step 1

📂 Servant of God

The Church begins to study the person's life, writings, and how they loved God and others.

Step 2

📜 Venerable

The Church says the person lived with “heroic virtue.” Kateri: 1943, by Pope Pius XII.

Step 3

🙏 Blessed

The person is “beatified” and can be called Blessed. Kateri: June 22, 1980, by Pope John Paul II.

Step 4

😇 Saint

The person is “canonized.” Kateri: October 21, 2012, by Pope Benedict XVI, in Rome.

✨ A Healing

Before someone is made a saint, the Church studies a miracle. For Kateri, it was the healing of a young boy from Washington State named Jake, from the Lummi Nation. In 2006 he became very sick from a dangerous infection. Many people prayed, asking Kateri to pray for him, and he got better. The Church accepted this as a miracle.

📅 Her Feast Day

A feast day is a saint's special day in the Church year. In the United States, we celebrate Saint Kateri on July 14. In Canada, her feast is April 17, the day she died.

🌳 Patron Saint

A patron saint is a saint people ask to pray for a special group or cause. Saint Kateri is a patron of ecology and the environment (caring for nature) and of Native peoples.

👪 For Grown-ups

What this teaches

  • The life of Saint Kateri Tekakwitha (1656–1680), presented respectfully from a Catholic perspective: faith, baptism, prayer, virtues, feast day, and the steps to canonization.
  • History and geography: Mohawk and Algonquin roots, 17th-century New York and New France, and her journey to Kahnawake.
  • Character: six virtues matched to real moments in her life, with simple ways a child can practice them.

Grade band

About grades 3–5. Read-aloud helps younger readers.

Accuracy notes

  • Most of what we know comes from two Jesuit priests who knew her, Claude Chauchetière and Pierre Cholenec. Dates such as the smallpox outbreak (about 1660–61) and her age at baptism are approximate.
  • The meaning of “Tekakwitha” is debated, so the lesson gives the common explanation and says so. Her last words (“Jesus, I love you”) and her scars fading after death come from those Jesuit accounts, and the lesson presents them as what people who were there wrote.
  • “First Native American saint” here means the first from the United States and Canada. Saint Juan Diego of Mexico, an Indigenous saint, was canonized in 2002.
  • The route on the journey map is approximate and marked that way. Historians agree she traveled about 200 miles north to Kahnawake in 1677.

Sensitive topics

Her parents and brother died of smallpox, and she was teased and pressured for her faith. Both are told gently. The historical accounts also describe very harsh penances she practiced; they are left out here as not age-appropriate.

Time

About 30–45 minutes, and it's easy to split into two sittings (story and journey; then virtues, cross, and quiz).

Follow-up activities

Make a real forest cross on a nature walk from two sticks and string, and pray together for someone. On July 14, celebrate her feast day with a nature clean-up (she's a patron of ecology). The Saint Kateri National Shrine is in Fonda, New York, if you're ever nearby.
