👋 Meet Joseph

🏚️ A Poor Family

Joseph Desa was born on June 17, 1603, in the small town of Cupertino (Copertino) in southern Italy. His father, a carpenter, died in debt, and the family was very poor. Tradition says Joseph was born in a stable, a little like Jesus.

😮 “Boccaperta”

Joseph was often lost in thought, and he wandered around with his mouth hanging open. Other kids nicknamed him “Boccaperta,” which means “open mouth” in Italian. He was called clumsy and slow, and he had a hard time in school.

🚪 Doors Closed… and One Opened

Joseph wanted to serve God as a friar. Some religious orders said no. The Capuchins took him, then sent him away after about eight months. Finally, the Conventual Franciscans let him work in their stable, and later he joined them.

⛪ Father Joseph

Studying was very hard for him, but he kept trying. In 1628 he was ordained a priest. People came to love him for his gentleness, his joy, and his deep prayer.

☁️ Reported Flights

Many people, over many years, reported seeing Joseph fall into deep prayer (called an ecstasy) and rise into the air. No one could test this in a science lab back then. The Church studied the witnesses' reports very carefully before declaring him a saint.

🏛️ Investigated and Moved

All the crowds and wonders worried church leaders. In 1638, the Inquisition (a church court) questioned him and found he had done nothing wrong. Still, he was moved from friary to friary and kept away from crowds. He obeyed patiently.

🕯️ His Last Home

Joseph spent his last years in Osimo, Italy, and died there on September 18, 1663. That's why his feast day is September 18.

👑 A Saint

He was beatified in 1753, and Pope Clement XIII declared him a saint in 1767. Today he is a patron saint of students, test-takers, pilots and air travelers, and astronauts!

🙏 Before a Test

📚 Why Students Ask for His Help

Joseph struggled with schoolwork, so many Catholic students ask him to pray for them before tests. Tradition tells that at one big exam, the bishop asked him about the one Bible passage he knew really well. At his last exam, the bishop was so pleased with the first students that he passed the whole group, Joseph included!

🙏 A Sample Prayer for Kids

(A sample written for this lesson. You can use your own words too!)

Dear God, thank you for my mind and for everything I've learned. Please help me stay calm, think clearly, and do my best.
Saint Joseph of Cupertino, you know what it feels like to find school hard. Please pray for me. Amen.

✏️ Pray AND Prepare

Prayer goes together with doing your part, just like Joseph kept studying. Before a test: 1. Study a little each day. 2. Get a good night's sleep. 3. Eat breakfast. 4. Take three slow breaths. 5. Read each question carefully. You've got this!

🚀 Patron of Flyers and Astronauts

Because people reported seeing him rise into the air, Joseph became a patron saint of pilots, air travelers, and even astronauts. A patron saint is someone people ask to pray for them in a special part of life.

👪 For Grown-ups

What this teaches

  • The life of St. Joseph of Cupertino (1603–1663), Conventual Franciscan friar, presented from a respectful Catholic perspective.
  • Perseverance, humility, and obedience; a healthy view of prayer and preparation before tests.
  • History skills: telling documented facts from pious tradition.

Documented vs. tradition

  • Well documented: born 1603 in Copertino to a poor family; the nickname “Boccaperta”; turned away/dismissed (including by the Capuchins, c. 1620); taken in by the Conventual Franciscans at the Grottella; ordained priest March 28, 1628; examined by the Inquisition at Naples in 1638 and cleared; moved among friaries (Assisi, Pietrarubbia, Fossombrone) and finally to Osimo (1657); died at Osimo, September 18, 1663; beatified 1753 by Benedict XIV; canonized July 16, 1767 by Clement XIII.
  • Reported by witnesses and accepted in the canonization process: his ecstasies and levitations. Many sworn testimonies were collected, and Prospero Lambertini (later Benedict XIV), a famously rigorous examiner, reviewed the cause. The lesson presents these as reports the Church found credible, not as laboratory-verified events.
  • Tradition / popular stories: birth in a stable; specific anecdotes such as broken dishes at the Capuchins; the details of the exam story (the passage he happened to know; the bishop passing the group). These are presented as tradition.
  • The sample prayer was written for this lesson and is clearly labeled as such.

Grade band & time

Grades 3–5; about 30–40 minutes. Read-aloud is on every card.

Talk about it

“When is a time you felt slow or clumsy? What helped you keep going?” and “Why do you think Joseph stayed gentle when people misunderstood him?”
