👋 Meet Father Junípero Serra

  • 👶 Born on November 24, 1713, in the village of Petra on the island of Mallorca, Spain. His name was Miguel José.
  • 📚 He became a Franciscan friar, took the name Junípero, and became a priest and a university professor.
  • ⛵ In 1749, he sailed to Mexico (then called New Spain) as a missionary, and walked to Mexico City despite a painful leg.
  • 🔔 Starting with San Diego in 1769, he led the founding of the first 9 of California's 21 missions.
  • ⛪ He died in 1784 at Mission San Carlos in Carmel, where he is buried.
  • 😇 In 2015, Pope Francis declared him a saint in Washington, D.C., the first canonization ever held in the United States.
✝️ What is a missionary? Someone who travels to a faraway place to share their faith. Father Serra spent most of his life as a missionary in Mexico and California.

🧱 Life at a Mission

🔔 The Bells

Mission bells rang to call people to prayer, to meals, and to work. The bells helped keep the day on schedule.

🧱 Adobe Buildings

Missions were built with adobe: bricks made of mud and straw, dried in the sun. Many roofs had red clay tiles.

🌾 Farms and Ranches

Missions grew wheat, corn, beans, and grapes, and raised cattle, sheep, and horses. Native people did most of this work.

⛪ Church and Learning

The church was the heart of each mission. Native people who lived there were taught the Catholic faith, Spanish, and trades like weaving, blacksmithing, and making candles and soap.

🛤️ El Camino Real

The “Royal Road” linked the missions. Many were about a day's journey apart. Parts of today's Highway 101 follow the old route.

🤔 Two Sides of the Story

To understand history, we listen to everyone's story. The missions meant very different things to different people.

💔 Hard Times for Native Californians

Long before the missions, many Native nations lived in California, each with its own language, beliefs, and way of life. When the Spanish missions and soldiers came, life changed a lot. Many Native people caught new diseases, like measles and smallpox, and many died. Many lost their land and their old ways of living.

🔒 Not Free to Leave

Native people who were baptized were expected to live and work at the mission. They usually were not allowed to leave whenever they wanted, and some who left were brought back and punished. Some Native people resisted and fought back.

🗣️ Different Views Today

Today, people see Father Serra in different ways. Many Catholics honor him as a holy missionary who gave his whole life to sharing his faith. Many Native Californians feel deep sadness about what the missions did to their families, and some disagreed with making him a saint. Both feelings are important to listen to.

⛪ Why the Church Honors Him

The Church honors Saint Junípero for his strong faith, his hard work, and his love for the people he served. He traveled thousands of miles, often on foot. He also spoke up to Spanish leaders to protect Native people from soldiers who treated them badly. Saints were real people who lived in their own times, and the Church does not say everything at the missions was right. Pope Francis has asked forgiveness for wrongs done to Native peoples of the Americas.

🌻 Still Here Today

California's Native nations are still here! The Kumeyaay, Tongva, Chumash, Ohlone, Salinan, Esselen, Acjachemen, and many other peoples keep their languages, songs, and traditions alive.

😇 The Road to Sainthood

Becoming a saint takes a long time! The Church carefully studies a person's life, and there are special steps along the way.

Step 1

📂 Servant of God

The Church begins to study the person's life, writings, and how they loved God and others.

Step 2

📜 Venerable

The Church says the person lived with “heroic virtue.” Serra: 1985, by Pope John Paul II.

Step 3

🙏 Blessed

The person is “beatified” and can be called Blessed. Serra: September 25, 1988, by Pope John Paul II.

Step 4

😇 Saint

The person is “canonized.” Serra: September 23, 2015, by Pope Francis, in Washington, D.C.

🇺🇸 A First for the U.S.

Pope Francis canonized Father Serra at the Basilica of the National Shrine of the Immaculate Conception in Washington, D.C., during his first visit to the United States. It was the first time a saint was ever canonized on U.S. soil.

📅 His Feast Day

A feast day is a saint's special day in the Church year. Saint Junípero Serra's feast day is July 1.

➡️ “Always Forward!”

A motto often linked to Father Serra is “Siempre adelante”, Spanish for “Always forward!” Pope Francis repeated it when he made Serra a saint.

👪 For Grown-ups

What this teaches

  • The life of Saint Junípero Serra (1713–1784), presented respectfully from a Catholic perspective: vocation, missionary work, feast day, and the steps to canonization.
  • California history and geography: the nine missions founded under his leadership, with founding years, the Native peoples of each place, and El Camino Real.
  • Historical thinking: an honest, gentle look at how the missions affected California's Native peoples, and why people today hold different views.

Grade band

About grades 3–5. Read-aloud helps younger readers.

Accuracy notes

  • “Founded by Serra” follows the usual count of the nine missions begun under his leadership. He personally founded most of them, but fellow friars he sent started San Francisco (Francisco Palóu, 1776) and Santa Clara (Tomás de la Peña, 1777).
  • Carmel began at Monterey in 1770 and moved to Carmel in 1771. San Diego moved a few miles inland in 1774.
  • Pope Francis canonized Serra without requiring a second approved miracle. The “Siempre adelante” motto is traditionally attributed to him.

Sensitive topics

The “Two Sides” section mentions deaths from disease, loss of land and culture, and that baptized Native people were not free to leave and were sometimes punished, all without graphic detail. Historically, Serra, like others of his time, approved of physical punishment at the missions. That isn't detailed for kids here, but older children may come across it, and Serra statues in several California cities were taken down or removed in 2020. Being nearby for questions is a good idea.

Time

About 35–50 minutes, and it's easy to split into two sittings (story and map; then mission life, two sides, and quiz).

Follow-up activities

Build a mini mission from sugar cubes or cardboard (a classic California 4th-grade project). On July 1, his feast day, look up which Native nation's land you live on and learn one thing about their culture today.
