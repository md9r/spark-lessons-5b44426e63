👋 Meet Bach

  • 🎂 Born in 1685 in the town of Eisenach, Germany.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 His family was FULL of musicians — dads, uncles, cousins, and brothers!
  • 🎹 He was an amazing organ player (the giant instrument with pipes in churches).
  • ⛪ For 27 years he was the music director for churches in Leipzig, writing new music almost every week.
  • 👶 He had 20 children! Sadly, many died young, but several grew up to be famous composers too.
  • 🏰 His style is called Baroque (say "ba-ROKE") music. He died in 1750.
"Wait — what does 'Bach' mean?" In German, Bach means "brook" — a little stream of water! 🏞️

🕵️ Music mystery! This minuet is in a music notebook Bach made for his wife, Anna Magdalena. For a long time everyone thought Bach wrote it. But music detectives found clues that it was probably written by his friend Christian Petzold! It's still known as one of "Bach's" most famous tunes.

🎶 Layer the Melodies!

🧵 Counterpoint means playing two or more different melodies at the same time so they fit together like a braid. A fugue is a puzzle-piece of counterpoint: one voice starts a tune, then other voices come in one by one copying it, while the first keeps going!

Here's a round (also called a canon): every singer sings the same tune ("Frère Jacques"), but each one starts a little later. Turn the singers on or off — even while it plays — and hear how the melodies stack up!

💡 Try this: Play just Voice 1. Then play all three. Can you still pick out each melody? That's what Bach's listeners did! Bach loved canons — in his famous Goldberg Variations, every third piece is a canon.
