👋 Meet Christopher Columbus

  • 🎂 Born around 1451 in Genoa, a busy port city in what is now Italy.
  • ⚓ He went to sea when he was young and became a skilled sailor. Later he worked with his brother making maps in Portugal.
  • 💡 His big idea: the Earth is round, so if you sail west, you can reach Asia and its spices and gold!
  • 📏 His big mistake: he thought the Earth was much smaller than it really is. Asia was much, much farther away.
  • 👑 The king of Portugal said no. But Queen Isabella and King Ferdinand of Spain said yes and paid for his trip in 1492.
  • ⛵ He sailed with three ships — the Niña, the Pinta, and the Santa María — and about 90 sailors.
"Is Columbus his real name?" Sort of! In Italian he was Cristoforo Colombo, and in Spanish he is called Cristóbal Colón.

🧭 Life on the Ship

There was no GPS in 1492! Sailors found their way with a compass, kept time with a sand glass, and figured out where they were with dead reckoning. Try all three!

🧭 Steer by Compass

A compass needle always points north. Columbus's big plan was simple: sail west. The wind keeps pushing your ship off course — keep the arrow in the green zone (West) to reach land!

Press Start to set sail!

⏳ Turn the Sand Glass

Ships used a sand glass (like an hourglass) that took half an hour to run out. A ship's boy had to turn it over every time, day and night, and call out so everyone knew the time. Eight turns made one 4-hour watch (work shift). Here, the sand runs super fast!

Turns: 0 of 8

📐 Dead Reckoning

Dead reckoning means figuring out where you are using math: how fast you're going × how long you've sailed = how far you went. Add the compass direction, and you can mark your spot on the map!

🤝 Two Sides of the Story

History is like a coin — it has more than one side. Here's the part of the story told by the people who were already there.

🏝️ People Already Lived There

The islands where Columbus landed were home to the Taíno people (say "tah-EE-no"). They had villages, farms, canoes, leaders, games, and art. Millions of people lived across the Americas.

🔍 "Discovered"?

Columbus didn't find an empty land. It was new to Europeans, but it was already home to many people. That's why many people now say he "arrived in" or "reached" the Americas.

💔 Hard and Sad Times

Columbus and the Spanish settlers treated many Taíno people very unfairly and cruelly. They forced people to work, took some as slaves, and brought new diseases the Taíno's bodies had never fought before. Many, many Taíno died.

🎉 Two Holidays

In the United States, the second Monday in October is Columbus Day. Many Italian Americans celebrate it with pride. Many places also celebrate Indigenous Peoples' Day to honor the first people of the Americas and remember their story too.

🌺 The Taíno Today

Taíno culture didn't disappear! Many people in Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic, and Cuba have Taíno ancestors, and we still use Taíno words like hammock, canoe, and hurricane.

💭 Think About It

Columbus was a brave and very skilled sailor. He also did things that hurt a lot of people. Both are true! How do you think the Taíno felt when strangers arrived and said their home now belonged to Spain?

🌍 Myth Buster: Flat Earth?

MYTH

"Everyone thought the Earth was flat, and Columbus proved it was round!"

You might hear this story, but it's not true.

FACT

Educated people already knew the Earth was round.

Ancient Greek thinkers figured it out more than 2,000 years earlier! A man named Eratosthenes even measured how big around it was, and he got pretty close. The real argument in 1492 was about the Earth's size. Columbus's critics said Asia was too far to reach by sailing west — and they were right! He only survived because the Americas were in the way.
