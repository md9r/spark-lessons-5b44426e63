Who helps the President?

One person cannot run a whole country alone. The President of the United States has a special team of helpers. Can you guess what that team is called?

That team is called the Cabinet! Each person in the Cabinet leads a big part of the government, called a department.

They meet with the President, give advice, and help carry out the laws. Let’s meet them!

🧠 What Is the Cabinet?

👥 A Team of Advisers

The Cabinet is the group of people who lead the big executive departments. They give the President advice and run their departments day to day.

📜 The Constitution Hint

The U.S. Constitution talks about “executive Departments” and says the President can ask their leaders for written opinions. But it never uses the word “Cabinet.” That name came later, from custom!

🇺🇸 The Vice President Too

Today, the Vice President is part of the Cabinet as well. The Vice President is ready to become President if needed, and also helps lead the Senate.

🗳️ Senate Says Yes

The President picks (nominates) each department head. Then the Senate must agree. The Constitution calls this “advice and consent.”

📜 Then and Now

🏛️ Washington’s First Cabinet

President George Washington started with just four helpers:

  • Thomas Jefferson — Secretary of State
  • Alexander Hamilton — Secretary of the Treasury
  • Henry Knox — Secretary of War
  • Edmund Randolph — Attorney General

That was in the early 1790s. The country was brand new!

🇺🇸 Today’s Cabinet

Today there are 15 executive departments, plus the Vice President. New departments were added as the country grew — for things like schools, roads, energy, veterans, and keeping people safe after hard times.

The newest big one is Homeland Security, created in 2002.

⚔️ A Name That Changed

Washington’s “Secretary of War” job later became part of what we call the Department of Defense. That is still the name written in the law. Starting in 2025, leaders also began using “Department of War” as another name for the same team. Both names mean the department that runs the armed forces.

    👪 For Grown-ups

    What this teaches

    • What the U.S. Cabinet is: the Vice President plus the heads of the 15 executive departments.
    • That the Constitution mentions “executive Departments” (Article II, Section 2 opinion clause) and Senate “advice and consent” for appointments, but never uses the word “Cabinet.”
    • Kid-friendly roles for each of the 15 departments; Washington’s first Cabinet of four; how a nominee is confirmed; a simplified line of presidential succession.

    Level and time

    Written for a young 8-year-old. About 15–20 minutes. Easy to split (what the Cabinet is + the 15 departments; then the games and quiz).

    Nonpartisan note

    This lesson names no current officeholders and takes no political position. Historical names (Washington, Jefferson, Hamilton, Knox, Randolph) appear only in the founding story.

    Accuracy notes and sources

    • 15 departments and titles follow 5 U.S.C. § 101 and USAGov / White House cabinet structure: State, Treasury, Defense, Justice (Attorney General), Interior, Agriculture, Commerce, Labor, Health and Human Services, Housing and Urban Development, Transportation, Energy, Education, Veterans Affairs, Homeland Security.
    • Defense / War naming (as of November 2026): The statutory name remains the Department of Defense / Secretary of Defense (National Security Act framework; succession lists on USAGov still say Secretary of Defense). Executive Order 14347 (5 September 2025) authorized “Department of War” / “Secretary of War” as a secondary title. Official defense.gov pages currently style the department and secretary that way. A full statutory rename would need Congress; as of this writing, that change was still being debated (e.g. in NDAA discussions) and is not treated here as complete. The lesson therefore teaches both names for the same department and explains the dual naming in this grown-ups note.
    • Constitution: Art. II, § 2 (opinion clause and Appointments Clause / advice and consent). National Constitution Center and Mount Vernon digital encyclopedia for the “Cabinet” as a custom and for Washington’s first four (Jefferson–State, Hamilton–Treasury, Knox–War, Randolph–Attorney General). Mount Vernon notes Madison popularized the word “cabinet” for the meetings.
    • Succession: USAGov order after the President: Vice President, Speaker of the House, President pro tempore of the Senate, then the Cabinet secretaries in the order their departments were created, beginning with the Secretary of State. The lesson’s order game uses the first six of that line (VP through Attorney General) to keep it short.
    • Homeland Security created in 2002 (after the September 11, 2001 attacks) — National Constitution Center.
    • Left out / hedged: Cabinet-rank officials who are not department heads (e.g. White House Chief of Staff, UN Ambassador) — the lesson sticks to the 15 departments + VP. Exact meeting schedules, recess appointments, and acting secretaries. Any claim that Congress has finished renaming Defense to War.
